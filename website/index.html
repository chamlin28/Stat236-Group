Method

Data, model and words

This page covers the data we used, the mixed-membership model we fit to it, and the way we pulled words out of the speeches.

1. The data

Votes

We used the 360 final-passage roll calls on bills and joint resolutions in the 118th House, from Voteview. For each pair of members, the agreement weight is the share of the votes both cast, Yea or Nay, on which they voted the same way. Absences are ignored. Members who voted on fewer than half the bills, 180 of 360, were dropped, which leaves 428 of 445. Every pair keeps its weight, and nothing is thresholded. The result is the weighted matrix S.

The full matrix S, members sorted by strongest community

Speeches

Floor speeches come from the daily edition of the Congressional Record on GovInfo, with parsing code adapted from the boundaries-congress package (van der Does and Kolchinsky, 2021). We kept House speeches that carry a member identifier, run at least 250 characters and were given before 1 November 2024, so nothing said after the election is used. That leaves 20,513 speeches from members in the model.

Each member's speeches are embedded with a sentence model and reduced to five principal components. These enter the model as node covariates. Members with under 1,000 words after cleaning stay in the model through their votes, but they get no word list, which leaves 373 members with word statistics.

An outside check

The left–right score on member cards and in the Findings is the first dimension of DW-NOMINATE, from the nominate_dim1 column of Voteview's 118th-Congress member file. Voteview fits it to each member's votes over a whole career, so it does not depend on our 360 bills. We never feed it into the model.

2. DCMM

We model the House with a degree-corrected mixed-membership model. Member i has a membership vector πi over K communities, and the edge weights have expectation Ωij = θiθj πi⊤ P πj. Since Sij is the frequency with which i and j vote alike, we treat S as a nearly noiseless estimate of Ω.

Estimation follows Mixed-SCORE (Jin, Ke and Luo), extended with node covariates as in Ji and Jin.

  1. SCORE. Take the top four eigenvectors of S, ordered by absolute eigenvalue, and divide the second through fourth by the first. The ratios R̂ cancel the degree parameters.
  2. Covariates. Standardise the five speech components and take the top four left singular vectors Ĥ. Rescale them so their total variability matches that of R̂ (the scale constant is 13.9), and stack the two blocks as Z = [R̂, τĤ] with τ = τ₀ · 13.9 and τ₀ = 1.
  3. Vertex search. Run k-means with 10K = 40 centres, then apply the successive projection algorithm to the centres to find the four corners v̂1, …, v̂4.
  4. Memberships. Write each row of Z as a convex combination of the corners by constrained least squares. Divide the weights by b̂1(k) to account for degree, then normalise each member's weights to sum to one.

Choices

K. The scree plot of S has a large gap after the second eigenvalue, which is the party split. The third and fourth, 7.0 and 2.7, are small but well above the noise bulk, so we used K = 4. K = 3 is defensible too. We ran no formal test, because SgnQ needs a binary graph.

τ₀. The speech block gets the same total spread as the vote block. That is a tuning choice, and the corners move when it changes.

The vote cutoff. Members with few shared votes have noisy agreement weights, so we required at least half the bills.

3. Obtaining the words

Each member's House floor speeches (no Extensions of Remarks, at least 250 characters, before 1 November 2024) are joined into one document. We count single words, and in the phrase version also two-word phrases, that at least 10 members use. English stop words, floor procedure terms such as "yield" and "gentleman", and state names are removed. Members with under 1,000 words after cleaning are left out of the word analysis, and the remaining members' counts are rescaled to the median document length, so a member who speaks often still counts once.

Community counts are weighted by membership. The count for community k is the sum over members of πik times member i's counts. To find the words that set a community apart, we compare it with everyone else using the weighted log-odds ratio with an informative Dirichlet prior (Monroe, Colaresi and Quinn, 2008), and keep words with z above 1.96. A word is dropped if a single member supplies more than 40% of a group's use of it. We also compare the two largest communities inside each party, which removes the plain Democrat-versus-Republican difference.

The lists on member cards use the same test, with the member as one group and the rest of the House as the other, and a word must appear at least three times in the member's speeches. The number beside each word is the member's rate per word spoken divided by the rest of the House's rate. Caucus, committee and nearest-community word clouds compare the group with everyone else in the same way, with member surnames removed and, for committees, the words in the committee's name.

One caveat. Speech also helped build the communities, so some of the vocabulary differences are built in.

Summary

The four communities separate the House more finely than party does. Most members carry weight on more than one community, each party divides into two, and a single left–right score separates the parties but does not separate the two Democratic communities from each other.

This covers one Congress, the 118th House. The communities summarise its votes and speeches, and the names are ours.

Limitations

  • Four communities is a judgment call. The scree plot supports three or four, and we ran no formal test.
  • Giving votes and speech equal weight (τ₀ = 1) is our choice, and the corners move when it changes.
  • Speech helps define the communities and also describes them, so some of the word differences are built in.
  • Floor speech is uneven. 55 of 428 members have under 1,000 words after cleaning and get no word list, and tributes to constituents put some local names into member word lists.
  • The community names are ours. The model produces only V1 through V4.
